## Introduction

The Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) is often presented as a measure of whether people can resist a tempting [first answer]({{ 'first-answer/' | relative_url }}) and stop to think. That interpretation captures part of what the test does, but it is incomplete. The classic CRT questions also require basic numerical reasoning, arithmetic fluency and an understanding of quantitative relationships. As a result, a low score does not automatically mean that someone failed to reflect. In some cases, the person may have recognised that the intuitive answer was suspicious yet still lacked the numerical skills needed to reach the correct solution. Research over the past decade has increasingly treated CRT performance as a mixture of reflection and numeracy rather than a pure measure of reflective thinking alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13546783.2017.1292954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[Taylor &amp; Francis Online+2ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taylor &amp; Francis OnlineThe cognitive reflection test revisited: exploring the ways...by B Szaszi · 2017 · Cited by 119 — Additionally, w...</span></span></span>

## Does the CRT Measure Reflection or Math?

Shane Frederick's original CRT was designed to measure the tendency to override an intuitive but incorrect response and engage in further reflection. The famous [bat-and-ball]({{ 'bat-and-ball/' | relative_url }}) problem succeeds because it generates an answer that feels right before arithmetic verification occurs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2F089533005775196732" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeaweb.org">[American Economic Association]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeaweb.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">American Economic AssociationCognitive Reflection and Decision Makingby S Frederick · 2005 · Cited by 8284 — This paper introduces a thre...</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that solving the problem correctly requires more than noticing the trap. A participant must also perform the relevant calculation. This creates an important measurement problem: the test cannot easily distinguish between someone who failed to reflect and someone who reflected but lacked the necessary numerical competence.

Researchers have repeatedly found substantial relationships between CRT scores and measures of numeracy, mathematical ability and quantitative reasoning. Some studies have argued that a significant portion of the CRT's predictive power may come from its numerical demands rather than from reflection alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260061139_The_Cognitive_Reflection_Test_how_much_more_than_numerical_ability" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2eScholarship]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">260061139 The Cognitive Reflection Test how much more than numerical ability</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) The Cognitive Reflection Test: how much more than...Frederick&#x27;s (2005) Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) is a 3-item tas...</span></span></span>

This does not mean the CRT is "really just a maths test". Rather, the evidence suggests that it blends at least two ingredients:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* The tendency to question an intuitive response.
* The ability to execute the reasoning needed to replace it.

</div>

A person needs both to achieve a high score.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13546783.2017.1292954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[Taylor &amp; Francis Online+2MOspace]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taylor &amp; Francis OnlineThe cognitive reflection test revisited: exploring the ways...by B Szaszi · 2017 · Cited by 119 — Additionally, w...</span></span></span>

## Why Arithmetic Skill Affects CRT Performance

The original CRT items appear simple, but each depends on quantitative concepts that not everyone handles equally well.

Consider the bat-and-ball problem. Someone may recognise that 10 cents cannot be correct because the numbers do not quite fit. Yet moving from that insight to the correct 5-cent answer requires forming and solving a simple numerical relationship. Similarly, the machine-and-widget problem depends on understanding rates, while the lily-pad problem requires reasoning about exponential growth rather than linear growth.

Researchers examining response patterns have found that errors do not always arise because participants blindly accept the intuitive answer. Some respondents reject the intuitive answer but still fail to reach the correct one. This suggests that inadequate numerical knowledge can be a separate source of failure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13546783.2017.1292954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[Taylor &amp; Francis Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taylor &amp; Francis OnlineThe cognitive reflection test revisited: exploring the ways...by B Szaszi · 2017 · Cited by 119 — Additionally, w...</span></span></span>

## What Numeracy Changes About Score Interpretation

The strongest critique of simplistic CRT interpretation is not that reflection is irrelevant. It is that reflection cannot be cleanly separated from numerical competence when using the classic items.

Several studies have concluded that CRT performance is best viewed as a multifaceted construct. Reflection matters, but so do numeracy and problem-solving ability. Research revisiting CRT performance has found evidence that both reflective tendencies and numerical skill independently contribute to scores.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13546783.2017.1292954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[Taylor &amp; Francis Online+2PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taylor &amp; Francis OnlineThe cognitive reflection test revisited: exploring the ways...by B Szaszi · 2017 · Cited by 119 — Additionally, w...</span></span></span>

This changes how a low score should be interpreted.

A low score may indicate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* A tendency to accept the first plausible answer.
* Weak arithmetic fluency.
* Difficulty with quantitative concepts such as ratios or growth rates.
* Some combination of all three.

</div>

Likewise, a high score does not necessarily prove exceptional reflective habits. Strong numerical skills can make the correct answer easier to reach once reflection begins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260061139_The_Cognitive_Reflection_Test_how_much_more_than_numerical_ability" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2Edith Cowan University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">260061139 The Cognitive Reflection Test how much more than numerical ability</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) The Cognitive Reflection Test: how much more than...Frederick&#x27;s (2005) Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) is a 3-item tas...</span></span></span>

The practical lesson for improving analytical thinking is important. If someone performs poorly on CRT items, the next question should not be "Are they reflective?" but "What kind of difficulty produced the error?" The answer may point to reasoning habits, mathematical knowledge, or both.

## Fairer Ways to Read a Wrong Answer

Looking only at whether an answer is correct throws away useful information.

Researchers studying CRT response patterns have noted that some participants generate neither the standard intuitive answer nor the correct answer. Others appear to reject the intuitive response but become stuck before reaching the solution. These patterns suggest that reflection and numeracy contribute in different ways.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13546783.2017.1292954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[Taylor &amp; Francis Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Taylor &amp; Francis OnlineThe cognitive reflection test revisited: exploring the ways...by B Szaszi · 2017 · Cited by 119 — Additionally, w...</span></span></span>

A fairer interpretation of a wrong answer asks three questions:

**Did the person immediately choose the intuitive answer?**

If so, the error may reflect insufficient checking or reflection.

**Did the person recognise the trap but fail to solve it?**

That pattern points more towards a numeracy limitation than a reflection limitation.

**Could the person [explain]({{ 'explain/' | relative_url }}) why the intuitive answer was wrong?**

Someone who can identify the flaw in the intuitive response may be demonstrating reflective thinking even if they cannot complete the calculation.

These distinctions matter because the educational response differs. Reflective weaknesses improve through habits of verification and deliberate checking. Numeracy weaknesses improve through [practice]({{ 'practice/' | relative_url }}) with quantitative reasoning and mathematical concepts.

## What the Evidence Means for Analytical Skill Development

The broader research record still supports the CRT as a valuable indicator of thinking style. Higher CRT scores [predict]({{ 'predict/' | relative_url }}) performance on many judgement and decision-making tasks, and the test remains one of the most influential tools in the study of reasoning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21541821/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed+2Springer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cognitive Reflection Test as a predictor of...by ME Toplak · 2011 · Cited by 1580 — In this study, we showed that the CRT is a...</span></span></span>

However, the evidence also warns against treating CRT scores as a pure measure of reflective thought. Studies examining the test's validity increasingly conclude that successful performance depends on multiple capacities, including numeracy, insight and reflection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326134901_The_Cognitive_Reflection_Test_A_Measure_of_IntuitionReflection_Numeracy_and_Insight_Problem_Solving_and_the_Implications_for_Understanding_Real-World_Judgments_and_Beliefs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate+2Taylor &amp; Francis Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) The Cognitive Reflection Test: A Measure of Intuition...19 Nov 2025 — The Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) has increasi...</span></span></span>

For anyone trying to improve analytical skills, the most useful takeaway is that reflection and numerical competence reinforce one another. Catching a first-answer trap requires the willingness to pause, but escaping it often requires the quantitative tools needed to evaluate alternatives. A wrong CRT answer therefore reveals less about a person's overall reasoning quality than many popular discussions suggest. It may be evidence of a reflection problem, a numeracy problem, or a mixture of the two. Understanding that distinction leads to a more accurate and more constructive reading of the score.
