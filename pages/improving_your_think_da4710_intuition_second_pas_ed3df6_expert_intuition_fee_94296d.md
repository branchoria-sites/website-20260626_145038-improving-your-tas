## Introduction

A useful rule for improving your thinking is that [intuition]({{ 'intuition/' | relative_url }}) should be trusted only after it has passed a [feedback loop]({{ 'feedback-loop/' | relative_url }}) test. The feeling of certainty is not enough. What matters is whether repeated experience has given you accurate, corrective feedback that allows your brain to learn reliable patterns.

Research on expertise shows that highly skilled intuition is real. Experienced firefighters, nurses, pilots, chess players, and craftspeople often recognise important signals before they can [explain]({{ 'explain/' | relative_url }}) them. However, the same research shows that intuition becomes unreliable when the environment is noisy, unpredictable, or slow to provide [feedback]({{ 'feedback/' | relative_url }}). The key question is not “Does this feel familiar?” but “Has this intuition been trained by reality?”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PubMedConditions for intuitive expertise: a failure to disagreeby D Kahneman · 2009 · Cited by 4040 — This article reports on an effort t...</span></span></span>

This distinction explains why some [gut feelings]({{ 'gut-feelings/' | relative_url }}) deserve respect while others require a deliberate second pass.

## When Should You Trust Expert Intuition?

Daniel Kahneman and Gary Klein approached intuition from different traditions. Kahneman became famous for documenting judgement errors and cognitive biases, while Klein studied experts making rapid decisions under [pressure]({{ 'pressure/' | relative_url }}). Despite their disagreements, they reached a notable point of consensus: expert intuition is trustworthy only under specific conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PubMedConditions for intuitive expertise: a failure to disagreeby D Kahneman · 2009 · Cited by 4040 — This article reports on an effort t...</span></span></span>

Their shared answer centred on two requirements:

1. The environment must contain stable patterns that can actually be learned.
2. The person must receive sufficient [practice]({{ 'practice/' | relative_url }}) and clear feedback about whether their judgments were correct. [PubMed+2LinkedIn](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/)

Without these conditions, experience alone can create confidence without improving accuracy. Someone may spend twenty years making forecasts, hiring staff, or picking investments and still fail to develop reliable intuition if the feedback is weak or misleading.

The feedback loop test therefore asks a simple question:

**Has reality repeatedly corrected this judgement in a way that allows learning?**

If the answer is unclear, caution is warranted.

## Kind Versus Wicked Learning Environments

Psychologist Robin Hogarth described the difference between “kind” and “wicked” learning environments. This distinction helps explain why expertise develops rapidly in some domains and poorly in others. [preview-prod-production.gurooproducer.com+2ResearchGate](https://preview-prod-production.gurooproducer.com/learntolead2021/hogarth-et-al-2015.pdf)

### Kind environments teach accurate intuition

A kind environment has several characteristics:

[* Patterns are relatively stable.](#endnote-5 "
<details class="endnote-snippet"><summary>Source snippet</summary><p>Hogarth characterized &#x27;kind&#x27; learning environments as situations that are governed by stable rules and repetitive patterns; feedback is q...&quot;)...</p></details>
* Cause and effect are connected.
* Feedback arrives quickly.
* Success and failure are visible.
* Similar situations occur repeatedly. [preview-prod-production.gurooproducer.com+2LinkedIn](https://preview-prod-production.gurooproducer.com/learntolead2021/hogarth-et-al-2015.pdf)

Chess is a classic example. A player makes a move and quickly discovers whether it improved or weakened the position. Tennis provides similar feedback. The player sees the result immediately and can adjust on the next point. Over thousands of repetitions, the brain learns dependable patterns. [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/experience-studio/202007/experience-kind-vs-wicked)

Many forms of emergency response also contain enough regularity to build intuition. Experienced firefighters often recognise danger because previous situations repeatedly taught them which cues mattered. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26798603_Conditions_for_Intuitive_Expertise)

### Wicked environments create misleading confidence

A wicked environment looks very different:

[* Feedback is delayed.](#endnote-1 "
<details class="endnote-snippet"><summary>Source snippet</summary><p>ResearchGate(PDF) Conditions for Intuitive ExpertiseKahneman and Klein (2009) show, for example, that intuitive expertise breaks down in&quot;)...</p></details>
* [Outcomes]({{ 'outcomes/' | relative_url }}) are influenced by luck. [* Important information is hidden.](#endnote-18 "
<details class="endnote-snippet"><summary>Source snippet</summary><p>In “wicked” learning environments often some information is hidden. Even when it isn&#x27;t, feedback...Read more&quot;)...</p></details>
* Causes and effects are difficult to separate.
* Success can reinforce the wrong lesson. [Driverless Crocodile+2YouTube](https://www.driverlesscrocodile.com/books-and-recommendations/david-epstein-on-kind-and-wicked-learning-environments/)

Financial markets illustrate the problem. An investor may make a poor decision and still earn money because the market rises. Another may make a sound decision and lose money because of events beyond their control. The outcome alone does not clearly teach the correct lesson. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26798603_Conditions_for_Intuitive_Expertise)

Long-term business forecasting often suffers from the same issue. Predictions may take years to resolve, and by the time results appear it can be difficult to identify which assumptions were right or wrong. The feedback loop is too weak to train intuition effectively. [ResearchGate+2McKinsey & Company](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26798603_Conditions_for_Intuitive_Expertise)

The danger is that people still accumulate experience in wicked environments. What they do not necessarily accumulate is expertise.

## Practice, Feedback, and Calibrated Confidence

Many people assume that confidence signals competence. Research on expertise suggests the opposite relationship is often more useful: confidence becomes informative only when it has been calibrated against repeated feedback. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/)

Consider two professionals:

* A radiologist who reviews thousands of scans and later learns whether the diagnosis was correct.
* A commentator who makes broad predictions about future economic trends and rarely receives precise scorekeeping.

Both may sound equally confident. Only one operates within a strong feedback system.

This is why deliberate practice matters. Repetition alone is insufficient. Improvement comes from repetition combined with correction. A musician improves by hearing mistakes. A surgeon improves through outcome tracking and review. An athlete improves because performance is measured continuously. Feedback closes the loop between action and reality. [merlin.rebrovic.net+2preview-prod-production.gurooproducer.com](https://merlin.rebrovic.net/blog/conditions-for-expert-intuition/)

Kahneman and Klein argued that evaluating intuition requires evaluating the environment in which it was formed. The central question is not whether someone is experienced but whether their experience occurred in a setting that supports genuine learning. [PubMed+2Semantic Scholar](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/)

### Why experience sometimes fails

Several factors can prevent intuition from improving:

* Outcomes are rare, making learning slow.
* Feedback arrives months or years later.
* Success depends heavily on chance.
* People remember wins more vividly than mistakes.
* Incorrect beliefs are never systematically checked. [ResearchGate+2Driverless Crocodile](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283828207_The_Two_Settings_of_Kind_and_Wicked_Learning_Environments)

Under these conditions, confidence can increase while accuracy remains unchanged. The person feels wiser because they have accumulated stories, but the stories have not necessarily taught valid lessons.

## Everyday Tests Before Trusting a Gut Feeling

Most decisions do not require extensive analysis. However, before relying on instinct in an important situation, it is worth running a few quick checks.

### Has this judgement been tested repeatedly?

Ask whether your intuition comes from many comparable situations with known outcomes.

A project manager who has overseen dozens of similar projects may have useful intuition about schedules. A first-time founder predicting the future of a new market probably has much weaker grounds for instinctive certainty.

### Did feedback arrive clearly and quickly?

The shorter the delay between judgement and outcome, the easier it is to learn.

Cooking, sports, and many technical skills provide rapid correction. Strategic forecasting, politics, and long-term investing often do not. [preview-prod-production.gurooproducer.com](https://preview-prod-production.gurooproducer.com/learntolead2021/hogarth-et-al-2015.pdf)

### Could luck explain the outcome?

A correct prediction is not always evidence of skill.

If a judgement succeeded once, ask whether the same reasoning would work repeatedly under similar conditions. One successful outcome may reflect chance rather than expertise.

### Is the environment stable?

Patterns learned in one environment may fail when conditions change.

A manager's instincts developed during rapid growth may become unreliable during recession. A clinician's experience with common cases may not apply to unusual presentations. Expertise is often more specific than people realise. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26798603_Conditions_for_Intuitive_Expertise)

### Have you kept score?

One of the simplest ways to improve intuition is to track predictions and decisions.

Recording expectations before outcomes are known creates a personal feedback system. Over time, it becomes easier to identify where intuition is genuinely accurate and where it consistently overreaches.

## The Practical Takeaway

The strongest intuition is not mysterious. It is the product of countless encounters with reality, each followed by information about what happened next. When feedback is rapid, accurate, and repeated, the brain becomes an effective pattern detector. When feedback is delayed, distorted, or absent, intuition can become little more than a persuasive feeling. [PubMed+2preview-prod-production.gurooproducer.com](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/)

For decisions that matter, the feedback loop test offers a useful filter: trust intuition most when it was trained in a kind environment with clear correction, and become more sceptical when the environment is wicked, uncertain, or resistant to learning. That distinction often separates genuine expertise from overconfident guesswork.
