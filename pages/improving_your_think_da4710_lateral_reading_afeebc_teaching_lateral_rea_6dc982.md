## Introduction

Students can learn to read laterally online, but they rarely acquire the habit simply by being told to “think critically”. Research from schools, colleges and teacher-development programmes shows that learners improve most when [lateral reading]({{ 'lateral-reading/' | relative_url }}) is taught as a concrete sequence of actions: leave the unfamiliar page, investigate the source elsewhere, compare [independent evidence]({{ 'evidence-routes/' | relative_url }}), and then return to judge the original claim. Rather than relying on intuition or scepticism alone, students learn a repeatable process that mirrors the practices of professional fact-checkers. Classroom studies suggest that even relatively short instructional programmes can produce measurable gains, although mastery requires repeated practice across subjects rather than a single lesson. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ovid.com/journals/jedup/fulltext/10.1037/edu0000740~lateral-reading-on-the-open-internet-a-district-wide-field" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ovid.com">[Ovid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ovid.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">edu0000740~lateral reading on the open internet a district wide field</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lateral Reading on the Open Internetby S Wineburg · 2022 · Cited by 261 — Students practiced the heuristic of lateral reading: leavin...</span></span></span>

## Can students learn to read laterally?

The strongest evidence comes from interventions that explicitly teach students to evaluate online information using the strategies employed by professional fact-checkers. Instead of inspecting only the appearance of a webpage, students are taught to investigate the people, organisations and evidence behind it by opening additional browser tabs.

This matters because many learners naturally adopt what researchers call "vertical reading": remaining on the original page, carefully inspecting its design, references and "About" section. While these features sometimes help, they are also easy to imitate. Lateral reading redirects attention towards independent information that the original publisher cannot control. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid%3Ayk133ht8603/Wineburg%20McGrew_Lateral%20Reading%20and%20the%20Nature%20of%20Expertise.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stacks.stanford.edu">[Stanford Digital Repository]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stacks.stanford.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Digital Repository Lateral Reading and the Nature of Expertise</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staying on a single...</span></span></span>

Across multiple educational settings—including secondary schools and universities—students have demonstrated improved ability to distinguish trustworthy sources from misleading ones after receiving structured instruction. Improvements are meaningful but incomplete: students become better evaluators, not perfect ones, reinforcing the idea that digital credibility is a skill developed through continued practice rather than a checklist that can be memorised once. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ovid.com/journals/jedup/fulltext/10.1037/edu0000740~lateral-reading-on-the-open-internet-a-district-wide-field" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ovid.com">[Ovid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ovid.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">edu0000740~lateral reading on the open internet a district wide field</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lateral Reading on the Open Internetby S Wineburg · 2022 · Cited by 261 — Students practiced the heuristic of lateral reading: leavin...</span></span></span>

## What classroom studies tested?

Several influential studies have examined whether lateral reading can be taught under ordinary educational conditions rather than in tightly controlled laboratory settings.

### District-wide high school implementation

One of the largest studies was conducted across a public school district in the United States. High-school government teachers received professional development before delivering six approximately 50-minute lessons on civic online reasoning. Students repeatedly practised leaving unfamiliar websites, searching for outside information, and evaluating sources before accepting claims.

Compared with students receiving regular instruction, participants became significantly better at judging the credibility of unfamiliar online sources. Importantly, the programme worked through classroom teachers rather than researchers directly teaching students, suggesting that the approach can be integrated into normal school practice when teachers receive appropriate support. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ovid.com/journals/jedup/fulltext/10.1037/edu0000740~lateral-reading-on-the-open-internet-a-district-wide-field" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ovid.com">[Ovid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ovid.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">edu0000740~lateral reading on the open internet a district wide field</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lateral Reading on the Open Internetby S Wineburg · 2022 · Cited by 261 — Students practiced the heuristic of lateral reading: leavin...</span></span></span>

### College students in an online course

Researchers also examined whether lateral reading could be taught in an asynchronous online university course during the COVID-19 pandemic. Students completed brief instructional modules demonstrating how professional fact-checkers investigate unfamiliar sources and then practised applying those methods to authentic online materials.

Students showed significant improvements in evaluating internet sources after the course. The findings were especially notable because instruction occurred entirely online without face-to-face teaching, suggesting that well-designed digital learning materials can successfully teach these habits. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/lateral-reading-college-students-learn-to-critically-evaluate-internet-sources-in-an-online-course/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu">[Misinformation Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Misinformation ReviewLateral reading: College students learn to critically...23 Feb 2021 — Lateral reading allowed fact checkers to eval...</span></span></span>

### Across different subjects

Later work has explored whether civic online reasoning belongs only in civics classes. Evidence suggests that similar lessons can be integrated into biology, history and other subjects by embedding credibility evaluation within ordinary disciplinary work rather than treating it as an isolated media-literacy exercise.

This cross-curricular approach reflects how students actually encounter online information: scientific claims, historical documents and current events all require evaluating unfamiliar digital sources. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1405276.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.eric.ed.gov">[ERIC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.eric.ed.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ERICCivic Online Reasoning Across the Curriculum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 2, 2023 — by S McGrew · 2023 · Cited by 80 — For example, as part of the workshop, a member of the research team demonstrated how to...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 2, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why practice and feedback matter

One consistent lesson from implementation research is that students improve because they repeatedly perform the actions of lateral reading—not because they merely learn its definition.

Successful lessons typically include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* teacher modelling of how an experienced reader investigates an unfamiliar source;
* guided practice with carefully chosen examples;
* discussion of why certain searches were useful;
* immediate feedback on students' reasoning;
* gradual movement from highly scaffolded tasks to independent evaluation.

</div>

Researchers found that students often know they should "be sceptical" but lack a practical procedure. Lateral reading fills this gap by giving learners explicit decisions to make when [uncertainty]({{ 'uncertainty/' | relative_url }}) arises. Instead of asking, "Do I trust this website?", they learn to ask, "Who runs this organisation?", "What independent information exists about it?", and "Do reliable sources support the claim?" <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cor.inquirygroup.org/curriculum/collections/teaching-lateral-reading/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cor.inquirygroup.org">[Inquiry Group]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cor.inquirygroup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inquiry GroupTeaching Lateral Reading &#124; CORIn this sequence of lessons, teachers model lateral reading and guide students through a serie...</span></span></span>

Feedback is especially important because beginners frequently search inefficiently. They may rely on the first search result, mistake popularity for credibility, or return too quickly to the original page. Classroom discussion helps students refine both their search strategies and their interpretation of what they find. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23328584231176451" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsCivic Online Reasoning Across the Curriculum: Developing...by S McGrew · 2023 · Cited by 80 — Taken together, these studies...</span></span></span>

## How lessons turn scepticism into action

An important contribution of lateral reading instruction is that it shifts students from a vague attitude of doubt to observable investigative behaviour.

Rather than encouraging blanket distrust, effective lessons teach students to pause only when encountering unfamiliar or consequential information. They then practise a sequence such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Pause before accepting the claim.
2. Open a new browser tab.
3. Investigate the publisher or author.
4. Look for independent reporting or expert sources.
5. Compare evidence across multiple credible sources.
6. Return to the original page with additional context.

</div>

Repeated use of this sequence helps transform credibility checking from an abstract idea into a routine habit. Students become quicker at recognising when further investigation is needed and more efficient at locating independent evidence. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cor.inquirygroup.org/curriculum/collections/teaching-lateral-reading/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cor.inquirygroup.org">[Inquiry Group+2Boosting]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cor.inquirygroup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inquiry GroupTeaching Lateral Reading &#124; CORIn this sequence of lessons, teachers model lateral reading and guide students through a serie...</span></span></span>

Teachers also commonly present paired examples—one credible source and one professionally designed but misleading source—to demonstrate that visual polish is a poor indicator of trustworthiness. Students learn that attractive websites, institutional logos and confident language cannot substitute for independent verification. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cor.inquirygroup.org/curriculum/collections/teaching-lateral-reading/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cor.inquirygroup.org">[Inquiry Group]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cor.inquirygroup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inquiry GroupTeaching Lateral Reading &#124; CORIn this sequence of lessons, teachers model lateral reading and guide students through a serie...</span></span></span>

## What implementation research suggests for schools

The growing body of research points towards several practical design principles.

First, teacher preparation matters. Studies that included professional development, modelling and shared lesson materials generally produced stronger classroom implementation than expecting teachers to invent their own approaches. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ovid.com/journals/jedup/fulltext/10.1037/edu0000740~lateral-reading-on-the-open-internet-a-district-wide-field" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ovid.com">[Ovid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ovid.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">edu0000740~lateral reading on the open internet a district wide field</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lateral Reading on the Open Internetby S Wineburg · 2022 · Cited by 261 — Students practiced the heuristic of lateral reading: leavin...</span></span></span>

Second, authentic examples appear more effective than artificial exercises. Students benefit from evaluating genuine websites, social media posts and public claims because these resemble the situations they face outside school. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cor.inquirygroup.org/curriculum/collections/teaching-lateral-reading/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cor.inquirygroup.org">[Inquiry Group]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cor.inquirygroup.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inquiry GroupTeaching Lateral Reading &#124; CORIn this sequence of lessons, teachers model lateral reading and guide students through a serie...</span></span></span>

Third, instruction should recur across the curriculum. Researchers consistently note that although short interventions improve performance, many students still struggle with difficult evaluation tasks. Continued opportunities to practise in different subjects are therefore more likely to build durable habits than isolated media-literacy units. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23328584231176451" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.sagepub.com">[Sage Journals]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.sagepub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sage JournalsCivic Online Reasoning Across the Curriculum: Developing...by S McGrew · 2023 · Cited by 80 — Taken together, these studies...</span></span></span>

Finally, [assessment]({{ 'assessment/' | relative_url }}) should reward reasoning rather than memorisation. Instead of asking students to recall a definition of lateral reading, successful programmes ask them to demonstrate how they would investigate an unfamiliar source and justify the decisions they make while doing so. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/measuring-what-matters-investigating-what-new-types-of-assessments-reveal-about-students-online-source-evaluations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu">[Misinformation Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>
