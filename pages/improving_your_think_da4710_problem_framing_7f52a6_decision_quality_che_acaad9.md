## Introduction

Before a major decision gains momentum, the most valuable question is often not “What should we do?” but “Are we solving the right problem in the right way?” Decision-quality checklists exist to test the problem frame before persuasive arguments, political commitments, or detailed analysis make the original framing difficult to challenge. Rather than predicting the correct answer, these checklists help decision-makers detect missing alternatives, [weak evidence]({{ 'weak-evidence/' | relative_url }}), hidden [assumptions]({{ 'assumptions/' | relative_url }}), and unjustified confidence. Research on decision quality consistently identifies framing, alternatives, information quality, and reasoning quality as core determinants of good decisions, regardless of the eventual outcome.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.decisionprofessionals.com/who-we-are/decision-quality" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decisionprofessionals.com">[Decision Professionals+2Rockflow]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decisionprofessionals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Decision ProfessionalsDecision QualityThis framework proposed captures the quality of decisions, with the evidence that all decisions sha...</span></span></span>

A useful checklist is therefore not a bureaucratic form. It is a structured attempt to answer a simple question: “If this frame is wrong, how would we know before committing resources?”

## Why Leaders Need Pre-Decision Checks

Many poor decisions begin with a frame that feels obvious. Once a team agrees that a problem is caused by one factor, evidence gathering often becomes a search for confirmation rather than a search for understanding. Studies of cognitive bias show that professionals remain vulnerable to [overconfidence]({{ 'overconfidence/' | relative_url }}), confirmation bias, anchoring, and related judgement errors even when they possess expertise and experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8763848/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Impact of Cognitive Biases on Professionals&#x27; Decision...by V Berthet · 2022 · Cited by 305 — First, the literature reviewed shows...</span></span></span>

This is why decision-quality practitioners distinguish between decision quality and outcome quality. A good outcome can result from a flawed process, just as a sound process can sometimes produce a disappointing outcome because of uncertainty. The quality check occurs before the outcome is known.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmctraining.com/articles-and-resources/decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmctraining.com">[PMC Training]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmctraining.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMC Training The Decision-Making Mistake That Costs Teams the Most</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Decision quality is an assessment of the reasoning process at the moment a decision was made.Read more...</span></span></span>

A pre-decision checklist is especially valuable when:

* The decision is expensive or difficult to reverse.
* Stakeholders strongly favour one explanation.
* Forecasts contain substantial uncertainty.
* Time [pressure]({{ 'pressure/' | relative_url }}) encourages premature agreement.
* The proposed solution emerged before the problem was carefully defined.

In these situations, the checklist acts as a forcing mechanism that slows commitment long enough to examine whether the frame itself deserves confidence.

## A Checklist Before the Big Decision

The following checklist focuses specifically on [testing]({{ 'testing/' | relative_url }}) the problem frame, not on evaluating detailed solutions.

### 1. Have We Defined the Purpose Clearly?

A frame should state what success looks like rather than merely describe what is currently frustrating.

Questions to ask:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* What decision are we actually trying to make?
* What outcome are we trying to improve?
* How will we know the problem has been solved?
* Are we describing a symptom or an underlying issue?

</div>

A useful warning sign is solution language embedded in the problem statement. For example, “How do we automate this process?” already assumes automation is the answer. A stronger frame asks what performance gap exists and what outcomes matter.

### 2. Have We Considered Genuine Alternatives?

Decision-quality frameworks consistently emphasise the importance of creative, workable alternatives because a decision with only one apparent option is often not a real decision.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.decisionprofessionals.com/who-we-are/decision-quality" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decisionprofessionals.com">[Decision Professionals+2Sokkeldirektoratet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decisionprofessionals.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Decision ProfessionalsDecision QualityThis framework proposed captures the quality of decisions, with the evidence that all decisions sha...</span></span></span>

Questions to ask:

* What are at least three fundamentally different ways to view this problem?
* If our preferred explanation were false, what else could [explain]({{ 'explain/' | relative_url }}) the situation?

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* What option would a sceptical outsider propose?
* What happens if we do nothing?

</div>

The objective is not to generate endless possibilities. It is to prevent the first framing from monopolising attention.

### 3. What Evidence Would Change Our Minds?

Weak frames often survive because teams collect supporting evidence but never identify disconfirming evidence.

Questions to ask:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* What facts would seriously challenge our current framing?
* Have we actively searched for contradictory evidence?
* Are we relying on anecdotes when broader data exist?
* Which assumptions are supported by evidence and which are merely plausible?

</div>

This check helps counter confirmation bias and congruence bias, both of which encourage people to test whether their preferred explanation is correct rather than whether competing explanations might fit better.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.professionalstandards.org.uk/sites/default/files/attachments/Advice%20on%20biases%20in%20fitness%20to%20practise%20decision-making%20models.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: professionalstandards.org.uk">[PSA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">professionalstandards.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">As is.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PSAAdvice on biases in fitness to practise decision-making...June 10, 2021 — Congruence bias – tied to confirmation bias this is where t...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 10, 2021</span></span></span>

### 4. Are We Confusing Precision with Certainty?

Decision-makers often become more confident than their evidence justifies. Research identifies overconfidence as one of the most common and persistent biases in professional judgement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8763848/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC+2Economic and Business Review]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Impact of Cognitive Biases on Professionals&#x27; Decision...by V Berthet · 2022 · Cited by 305 — First, the literature reviewed shows...</span></span></span>

Questions to ask:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Which numbers are estimates rather than measurements?
* What confidence range accompanies our forecasts?
* Are we using precise figures that imply more certainty than exists?
* What assumptions would have to remain true for our prediction to hold?

</div>

A forecast stated as “12.7%” may appear rigorous while resting on highly uncertain assumptions. The checklist should force discussion of uncertainty, not merely calculations.

### 5. Have We Examined the Frame from Other Perspectives?

Problem frames determine what gets included and excluded from consideration. Decision education research describes a helpful frame as one that clarifies purpose, scope, and perspective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.decisioneducation.org/decision-chain/helpful-frame" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decisioneducation.org">[decisioneducation.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decisioneducation.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Helpful FrameA decision frame has three components: (1) Purpose—what we hope to accomplish by this decision; (2) Scope—what to include an...</span></span></span>

Questions to ask:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Who experiences this problem differently?
* Which stakeholders would describe the issue in another way?
* What information sits outside our current scope but may matter?
* What would the problem look like from the customer's, citizen's, employee's, or competitor's perspective?

</div>

A frame that survives multiple viewpoints is generally stronger than one that depends on a single perspective.

## Testing Alternatives and Evidence Quality

The strongest framing checks combine alternative generation with evidence review rather than treating them as separate activities.

One practical technique is to create competing problem statements and compare the evidence supporting each. For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Customer dissatisfaction is caused by slow service.
* Customer dissatisfaction is caused by unclear expectations.
* Customer dissatisfaction is caused by inconsistent quality.

</div>

The team then asks which evidence supports each frame and what evidence contradicts it. This prevents the discussion from becoming a contest between advocates of one preferred explanation.

Another useful approach is the pre-mortem. Developed by cognitive psychologist Gary Klein, a pre-mortem asks participants to imagine that a proposed plan has already failed and then identify the reasons why. By assuming failure rather than merely discussing possible risks, teams often surface concerns that would otherwise remain unspoken. Research and practitioner accounts suggest that this “prospective hindsight” approach improves the identification of weaknesses and hidden assumptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hbr.org">[Psychology Today+3Harvard Business Review+3garyklein]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hbr.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">performing a project premortem</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Harvard Business ReviewPerforming a Project PremortemReprint: F0709A In a premortem, team members assume that the project they are planni...</span></span></span>

For [problem framing]({{ 'problem-framing/' | relative_url }}), the pre-mortem question becomes:

> “Suppose we discover in two years that we framed the problem incorrectly. What did we overlook?”

The answers often reveal missing stakeholders, ignored constraints, weak evidence, or untested assumptions.

## Using Checklists Without Turning Them Into Bureaucracy

A common criticism of decision checklists is that they can become mechanical exercises completed merely to satisfy a process requirement. That risk is real. Aviation and medicine demonstrate that checklists work best when they focus attention on critical failure points rather than attempting to capture every detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A937319/FULLTEXT02.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: diva-portal.org">[DIVA Portal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">diva-portal.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DIVA Portal Systematically Irrational Decision Making</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DIVA PortalSystematically Irrational Decision MakingJune 17, 2016 — by J Duintjer · Cited by 1 — a 12-question checklist which decision m...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 17, 2016</span></span></span>

Several principles help preserve their value:

**Keep them short.** A framing checklist should usually fit on one page. If it requires extensive explanation, people stop using it.

**Use questions, not boxes.** The goal is discussion. Questions reveal assumptions more effectively than yes-or-no compliance checks.

**Apply more scrutiny to bigger decisions.** A minor operational choice does not need the same framing review as a strategic investment.

**Encourage challenge.** A checklist loses value if participants believe that questioning the frame is politically risky.

**Review before commitment.** The ideal moment is before resources, reputation, or public promises become tied to a particular answer.

The purpose of a decision-quality checklist is not to guarantee correctness. It is to increase the odds that reasoning begins from a frame that has survived serious testing.
