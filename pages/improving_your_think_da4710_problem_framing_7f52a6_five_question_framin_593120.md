## Introduction

Before collecting evidence, it is worth spending a few minutes [testing]({{ 'testing/' | relative_url }}) whether the question itself is worth answering. Many reasoning failures begin not with bad evidence, but with a vague, incomplete or misleading problem statement. Research on decision framing and problem structuring consistently shows that the way a problem is defined influences what information people seek, which options they consider and what they ultimately decide. A better question makes evidence gathering faster, more relevant and less vulnerable to confirmation bias.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theuncertaintyproject.org/tools/decision-framing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theuncertaintyproject.org">[The Uncertainty Project+2PubsOnline]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theuncertaintyproject.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Uncertainty ProjectDecision FramingEffectively frame a decision in a clear, concise, and objective way to set up the process for prod...</span></span></span>

A practical way to improve framing is to run a simple five-question test before collecting any evidence. The goal is not to find the perfect wording. It is to transform a loose concern into a decision-ready question that can guide useful investigation.

## Current Condition and Desired Condition

The first two questions establish whether there is a meaningful gap to investigate.

**Question 1: What is happening now?**

Describe the current condition in observable terms. Avoid explanations, blame or proposed solutions. Focus on what can actually be seen.

Weak framing:

* Why is our software platform failing?

Stronger framing:

* Customer support requests have increased by 35% in three months and renewal rates have fallen.

The stronger version separates observation from interpretation. Evidence collection can now begin from a clear starting point rather than an assumption.

**Question 2: What would a better condition look like?**

Reasoning improves when the destination is explicit. A desired condition creates a reference point for judging evidence and evaluating options.

Weak framing:

* How do we improve customer experience?

Stronger framing:

* What changes would reduce support requests while maintaining renewal rates above the previous year's level?

Decision-framing research emphasises the importance of clarifying desired [outcomes]({{ 'outcomes/' | relative_url }}) and decision criteria before evaluating alternatives. Without a defined target, evidence gathering often becomes unfocused and endless.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theuncertaintyproject.org/tools/decision-framing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theuncertaintyproject.org">[The Uncertainty Project+2decisioneducation.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theuncertaintyproject.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Uncertainty ProjectDecision FramingEffectively frame a decision in a clear, concise, and objective way to set up the process for prod...</span></span></span>

## The Gap, Affected People, and Decision Need

Once the current and desired conditions are clear, three further questions determine whether the issue is actionable.

**Question 3: What gap actually needs explanation or action?**

The gap is the difference between the current condition and the desired condition.

Examples:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* Sales are lower than target.
* Waiting times exceed service standards.
* Project delivery is slower than required.

</div>

This question prevents a common mistake: collecting information about everything instead of investigating the specific discrepancy that matters.

**Question 4: Who or what is affected by the gap?**

Many poorly framed questions ignore stakeholders or affected systems.

Compare:

* Why are meetings inefficient?

with:

* Why are project teams failing to reach decisions in meetings, causing rework and delays for engineering and operations staff?

The second version immediately sharpens evidence gathering. Different stakeholders may experience different aspects of the same problem. Problem-structuring approaches were developed partly because complex situations often involve multiple affected groups with different perspectives and priorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/inte.26.6.117" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubsonline.informs.org">[PubsOnline]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubsonline.informs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">What&#x27;s the Problem? **Question 5: What decision depends on the answer?**<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://decisionframeworks.com/blog/what-is-decision-framing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: decisionframeworks.com">[decisionframeworks.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">decisionframeworks.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">what is decision framing</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">?27 Jul 2024 — Framing is about the choices we make in what we say, how we say it, what we emphasize, and what we leave unsaid, and how t...</span></span></span>

This is the most important test.

If no decision depends on the answer, the investigation may not be worth conducting.

Examples:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Which staffing model should we adopt?
* Should we redesign the onboarding process?
* Should we continue, expand or stop a programme?

</div>

A decision-focused question prevents evidence gathering from turning into information accumulation. Decision-quality frameworks treat a useful frame as one of the foundations of good judgement because it determines what information is relevant in the first place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_quality" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Decision quality</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Decision quality</span></span></span>

## The Five Questions Working Together

The test can be applied quickly to almost any issue.

Consider the complaint:

"Employee morale is low."

Running the framing test produces:


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/improving_your_think_da4710_problem_framing_7f52a6_five_question_framin_593120-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Framing Test illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/improving_your_think_da4710_problem_framing_7f52a6_five_question_framin_593120-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/improving_your_think_da4710_problem_framing_7f52a6_five_question_framin_593120-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. What is happening now? Employee engagement survey scores have fallen for two consecutive quarters.
2. What would better look like? Engagement scores return to the previous year's level.
3. What gap matters? The decline in engagement over the last six months.
4. Who is affected? Employees, team leaders and retention outcomes.
5. What decision depends on the answer? Whether management should change workload allocation, communication practices or career development policies.

</div>

Notice what has happened. The problem has not been solved, but it has become specific enough to guide evidence collection. The search for information now has boundaries and purpose.

## How to Tell If the Question Is Still Too Vague

Even after using the five-question test, some problem statements remain weak. Several warning signs indicate that further framing work is needed.

### The question already contains the solution

Questions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* How do we automate this process?
* How do we hire more staff?
* How do we introduce artificial intelligence?

</div>

assume the answer before evidence has been collected.

A better frame focuses on the underlying gap:

* What is causing processing delays, and which intervention would reduce them most effectively?

This distinction matters because framing can subtly bias the options people consider. ### The scope is too broad

Questions such as:

* How do we improve education?
* How do we improve company performance?

have no practical boundary.

Evidence gathering becomes inefficient because almost any fact might seem relevant.

A stronger version narrows the decision:

* Which interventions would improve reading attainment among Year 7 pupils in schools with declining literacy scores?

### Success cannot be recognised

If nobody can [explain]({{ 'explain/' | relative_url }}) what improvement would look like, evidence cannot be evaluated properly.

Questions that lack measurable or observable outcomes often produce endless debate because participants are judging different goals without realising it.

### Different people are answering different questions

One of the strongest indicators of poor framing is disagreement about what problem is being discussed.

For example:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Finance sees a cost problem.
* Operations sees a process problem.
* Customers experience a service problem.

</div>

Problem-structuring research emerged precisely because groups frequently fail to agree on the nature of the problem before attempting analysis. ## Why This Test Improves Evidence Collection

The five-question framing test works because it changes the role of evidence.

Without framing, people often collect information that supports existing beliefs or preferred solutions. With framing, evidence serves a specific decision need.

Instead of asking:

* What information can I find?

the thinker asks:

* What information would help determine the size of the gap, identify affected parties and support the decision that must be made?

This shift reduces wasted effort, narrows the search space and makes it easier to notice missing information. It also creates a natural defence against confirmation bias because the frame is established before the evidence hunt begins. Well-structured decision and [problem-framing]({{ 'problem-framing/' | relative_url }}) approaches consistently emphasise this sequence: define the problem first, then collect information that is relevant to that definition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theuncertaintyproject.org/tools/decision-framing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theuncertaintyproject.org">[evolvingorganisation.co+3The Uncertainty Project+3Mural]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theuncertaintyproject.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Uncertainty ProjectDecision FramingEffectively frame a decision in a clear, concise, and objective way to set up the process for prod...</span></span></span>

3. What specific gap needs explanation or action?
4. Who or what is affected?
